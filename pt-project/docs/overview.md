# Information and Communication System (Secondary School Model Case Study)

An Information and Communication system comprising of voice and data distribution service has been designed in this project using a secondary school model as a case study. It involved identification, determination and specification of data communication products and services that best satisfy user need in a way that gives room for network scalability, redundancy and effective manageability. The project also focuses on the design of an enterprise network by effectively deploying technologies and protocols as Voice over IP, Access Control Lists, EIGRP routing, Fibre Optics, VLSM for addressing, Inter VLAN routing, Network Address Translation, use of DHCP and wireless routing. This project should be useful to Network Communications Engineers as it covers the major requirement that necessitates an enterprise network.

Keywords: VLSM, VoIP, Routing, Network Address, Translation

### Background of The Study (1.2)
This project focuses on Enterprise Network simulation and the design for Rural and Community Banks in Ghana; Adonten Rural Bank Limited is the area of study. In the digital age, robust and efficient enterprise network infrastructure is a critical enabler of business operations, particularly for financial institutions such as banks that handle sensitive data and provide real-time services. Mumuadu Rural Bank, like many rural financial institutions in Ghana and other developing regions, faces increasing demands for secure, fast, and reliable data communication across its branches and service centers. These demands are driven by the growing adoption of digital banking services, mobile banking platforms, inter-branch transactions, and the need for real-time data synchronization. Traditionally, many rural banks operate on basic IP-based networks, which often suffer from limitations such as high latency, poor scalability, weak Quality of Service (QoS), and vulnerability to congestion and security threats. These limitations become more pronounced as customer expectations grow and the complexity of financial services increases. Mumuadu Rural Bank, in its quest to modernize and improve service delivery, must therefore transition from legacy network systems to more intelligent and efficient network architectures. Multi-Protocol Label Switching (MPLS) is a high-performance method for forwarding packets through a network that combines the speed of switching with the intelligence of routing. It enables more efficient data forwarding, better traffic engineering, and improved support for Quality of Service and Virtual Private Networks (VPNs). For banks like Mumuadu, which require high reliability, security, and performance in their network systems, MPLS presents a promising solution. This study is motivated by the operational challenges currently faced by Mumuadu Rural Bank due to its existing network architecture, including delays in transaction processing, difficulty in managing network traffic, and limited scalability. By designing an improved enterprise network incorporating MPLS, the study seeks to address these issues and propose a network model that enhances performance, ensures data security, and supports future expansion.  Through this case study, the research will explore how MPLS can be tailored to meet the specific needs of rural banking institutions, identify key implementation considerations, and evaluate performance improvements using relevant network metrics. The outcome of this study aims to serve as a reference model for other rural banks and institutions seeking to adopt modern networking technologies for better service delivery and operational efficiency. As financial institutions expand their digital footprint, there is a growing need for a network infrastructure that is not only reliable but also scalable and secure. Mumuadu Rural Bank has continued to grow its operations by opening new branches and embracing electronic banking services to better serve its customers. However, this expansion places a significant strain on its existing network infrastructure, which was not originally designed to handle such complex and high-volume data transactions.
Issues such as poor network response times, intermittent connectivity between branches, and inefficient bandwidth utilization have increasingly hindered day-to-day banking operations. These challenges not only affect internal communication and data exchange but also compromise customer service delivery, delay transactions, and reduce the bank's competitiveness in a digital financial ecosystem.
MPLS, with its ability to create end-to-end circuits across any type of transport medium using label-switching techniques, offers a compelling alternative to traditional IP routing. By allowing more efficient routing and prioritization of data packets, MPLS enhances traffic engineering and ensures that critical banking applications—such as core banking systems, online banking platforms, and transaction processing services—receive the necessary bandwidth and low-latency paths to function optimally. Furthermore, MPLS supports the implementation of VPNs, enabling secure communication between Mumuadu Rural Bank’s headquarters and its branch offices. This capability is essential in the banking industry, where data confidentiality, integrity, and availability are paramount. With MPLS, the bank can also manage network resources more intelligently by defining policies that control traffic flow, ensuring that vital services remain unaffected even during periods of high network demand.
In addition to performance benefits, the adoption of MPLS can help reduce operational costs in the long term by simplifying network management, enhancing scalability, and minimizing downtime. These advantages make MPLS an attractive option for financial institutions like Mumuadu Rural Bank that are seeking to modernize their infrastructure in a cost-effective and future-proof manner. The need for this study is therefore underscored by the bank’s current limitations and the broader trend toward digital transformation in the financial sector. By designing and simulating an improved enterprise network model with MPLS for Mumuadu Rural Bank, this research aims to demonstrate how such an approach can solve existing challenges and lay the groundwork for a more resilient, efficient, and secure network architecture.

### Problem Statement (1.3)
In today’s fast-paced and digitally driven business environment, enterprise networks face increasing demands for high performance, scalability, security, and reliability. Traditional IP-based routing mechanisms often fall short in meeting these demands, especially when handling large volumes of data, real-time applications, and multiple service types across geographically dispersed locations. These limitations result in increased latency, network congestion, inefficient bandwidth utilization, and poor Quality of Service (QoS).
Multi-Protocol Label Switching (MPLS) has emerged as a promising technology to address these challenges by streamlining traffic flows and enabling more efficient routing decisions. However, many existing enterprise network architectures are not optimized to fully leverage the benefits of MPLS, such as enhanced traffic engineering, faster packet forwarding, and improved support for converged services (voice, video, and data).
This project aims to design an improved enterprise network architecture that integrates MPLS to overcome the limitations of traditional networks. The goal is to enhance overall network performance, optimize resource usage, and ensure scalable and secure communication across enterprise sites. By addressing current inefficiencies, the proposed design seeks to provide a robust solution that supports future growth and evolving business requirements. 
Despite the proven advantages of MPLS, many enterprise networks continue to rely on conventional IP routing protocols such as OSPF and EIGRP, which are not inherently optimized for prioritizing critical traffic or ensuring predictable performance. These traditional networks lack the flexibility required to dynamically adapt to changing traffic patterns, making them vulnerable to bottlenecks, packet loss, and inconsistent service delivery—especially in scenarios involving high-bandwidth or latency-sensitive applications like video conferencing, cloud computing, and VoIP services.
Moreover, the lack of proper network segmentation and traffic engineering capabilities in legacy systems makes it difficult to guarantee service-level agreements (SLAs), enforce security policies effectively, or achieve seamless integration with emerging technologies such as Software-Defined Networking (SDN) and cloud services. This situation poses a significant risk to business continuity, operational efficiency, and customer satisfaction.
An improved enterprise network design that incorporates MPLS can address these challenges by enabling better control over traffic flows, facilitating Virtual Private Network (VPN) implementation, and ensuring end-to-end QoS. Additionally, MPLS supports scalability and redundancy, which are critical for large organizations with multiple branches or data centers.
This project, therefore, seeks to develop and simulate an enhanced enterprise network model using MPLS to demonstrate its performance benefits compared to conventional architectures. The study will focus on evaluating key network performance metrics such as throughput, latency, packet loss, and bandwidth utilization. The outcome will provide practical insights and a viable framework for organizations aiming to modernize their network infrastructure for improved efficiency, reliability, and future-readiness.

### Scope of the project (1.5)
The proposed project covers four branch offices and a head office, with the head office designated to function as the central data center. Each branch will be equipped with a mast to mount radio antennas, enabling point-to-point wireless connectivity. The height of each mast is determined based on the geographical landscape surrounding the respective branch, and the masts will be fabricated by specialized welders. Barring any natural disruptions, the installation of each mast is estimated to take approximately 30 working days.
To ensure the successful design and simulation of the enterprise network, the project will require the expertise of key technical personnel, including a Network Architect, Network Administrator, and System Administrators. The Network Architect will be responsible for conducting a line-of-sight analysis using tools such as Google Earth to plot coordinates from the central station (head office) to each branch location. The Network Administrator will handle the subnetting of IP addresses, as well as the configuration of routers, switches, and radio antennas to establish stable connectivity. Subnetting, which involves dividing a larger IP network into smaller, manageable sub-networks (subnets), is a fundamental practice in network design. It enhances network performance and security by organizing IP address allocation efficiently (source).

### Limitations of the Project (1.6)
Time Constraints: The project is expected to be completed within a three-month timeframe. However, unexpected delays due to weather conditions, equipment availability, or logistical challenges—particularly during mast construction—could extend the project schedule.
Budgetary Limitations: The total estimated cost of GH₵60,000.00 may constrain the selection of premium equipment or technologies. Any price fluctuations or unforeseen expenses may lead to compromises in hardware quality or scope coverage.
Geographical and Environmental Factors: The effectiveness of the point-to-point radio connectivity heavily depends on line-of-sight availability. Varying topographical features across the bank’s branch locations may require custom mast heights and placement strategies, which could introduce additional complexity and costs.
Technical Resource Constraints: The project relies on the availability and expertise of key personnel such as a Network Architect, Network Administrator, and System Administrators. Any shortage in skilled labor or miscommunication among technical teams could affect the quality and speed of network configuration and deployment.
